# CASE-004B — Trigger & Transmission Reconstruction V0

Status: PROTOCOL DRAFT / EMPIRICAL RECONSTRUCTION NOT STARTED

## Research objective

Identify recurring financial-system vulnerabilities, observable
stress transitions, triggers, amplification mechanisms, and
resilience conditions across different banking crises.

Goal: derive and externally test a conditional cross-crisis
mechanism model. No universal sequence is assumed.

## Existing evidence carried forward

- Credit/GDP expansion: strongest recurring leading-indicator
  candidate in CASE-004B.
- LTD and real house prices: accompanying vulnerability signals.
- Bank capital and noncore funding: inconsistent discriminators.
- Correlation and temporal precedence do not prove causality.
- Existing findings and checkpoints remain authoritative.

## Fixed initial cases

Crisis:
1. USA 2007 — JST-marked banking crisis
2. SWE 1991 — JST-marked banking crisis
3. JPN 1997 — JST-marked banking crisis

Comparators:
4. CAN 2008 — JST non-crisis reference year
5. AUS 2006 — JST non-crisis reference year

These are deliberately heterogeneous exploratory cases,
NOT randomly sampled cases or final independent validation.

Comparator status must be verified for the full follow-up
horizon and the relevant financial-system stress measures.
No JST marker is not proof of financial resilience.

## Evidence sequence for each case

A. PRE-EXISTING VULNERABILITY:
   What was observable before the first stress event?

B. EARLIEST DOCUMENTED STRESS:
   What visibly deteriorated first, and when?

C. CANDIDATE TRIGGER:
   What event is hypothesized to have shifted the system
   from vulnerability toward active financial instability?

D. TRANSMISSION / AMPLIFICATION:
   Through which balance sheets, funding markets,
   counterparties or asset prices did distress spread?

E. OUTCOME / ABSORPTION:
   What measurable stress, losses, credit contraction,
   policy response or stabilization followed?

Ordering A-E is an investigative framework,
not an assumed chronological result.
Overlapping, reversed, or unidentified stages are allowed.

## Evidence classification

OBSERVED: fact directly supported by dated primary source.
INFERRED: mechanism interpretation with explicit assumptions.
UNKNOWN: unavailable or insufficient evidence.
CONTRADICTED: direct evidence against proposed mechanism.

For each factual observation retain:
- exact source URL and institution
- original publication date
- event date / observation period
- quantitative measure and unit, if available
- evidence classification
- independent alternative explanation

Primary sources preferred:
central-bank archives, supervisory reports, official inquiry
reports, financial statements and transaction disclosures.

Secondary academic research may identify candidates but
must not replace verification of underlying observations.

## Testing rules

1. Do not infer causal transmission from chronology alone.
2. Do not silently treat bank rescue dates as stress onset.
3. Do not assume identical mechanisms across cases.
4. Record contradictory observations and missing links.
5. Compare relevant shocks and institutional conditions,
   not just crisis versus non-crisis labels.
6. Require stronger identification or falsification tests
   before claiming a causal effect.
7. Reserve additional cases for later model validation.
8. Keep revised-vintage/PIT and missing-data audit open.
9. Do not alter GCF or frozen production evidence.
