# CASE-004B — Joint Vulnerability & Resilience Checkpoint V1

Status: INTERIM / EXPLORATORY — NOT FINAL

## Research objective

Identify recurring leading indicators of systemic banking crises,
their temporal ordering, and the conditions under which financial
vulnerability develops into a crisis rather than being absorbed.

## Established historical candidate

Credit/GDP expansion remains the strongest recurring leading
indicator candidate in the existing CASE-004B evidence.

This is an association finding, not a universal causal rule.

## Joint signal observations

Modern crisis/control pathway comparison excluding GFC:
- 9 fully comparable country-crisis pairs
- Credit/GDP increase greater in 9/9
- LTD increase greater in 8/9
- Real house-price increase greater in 8/9
- All three greater in 7/9

Controls were selected retrospectively and are not
prospective prediction controls.

## Sequence investigation

Peak annual increase timing differs across crises.
No universal Credit/GDP -> LTD -> Housing order established.

Peak timing does not identify first warning onset
or causal transmission.

## Triple-boom risk set

Modern 1981-2017 country-year sample:
- 504 eligible country-years
- Triple positive changes: 206 observations
- Crisis within 3 years: 40/206 = 19.42%
- Other observations: 26/298 = 8.72%

These are overlapping country-year outcome windows,
not independent crisis events or live predictions.

## Episode-level audit

Consecutive observed triple-boom years:
- 206 country-years consolidated into 53 episodes
- 11 episodes linked to distinct crisis events within
  the five-calendar-year window (start through start+4)
- 40 episodes without crisis markers in that window
- 2 episodes right-censored

No crisis within five years is not permanent resilience.
Episode starts are not validated signal onset dates.
Long episodes can subsequently lead to crises.

## Funding structure pilot

Among 51 classifiable episodes:
- 11 crisis within five years
- 40 no crisis within five years

Noncore level at t-1 median:
- Crisis: 47.263
- No crisis: 50.047

Noncore change over t-5 to t-1 median:
- Crisis: 3.164
- No crisis: 2.580

Excluding GFC-overlapping windows:
- Crisis: 7 episodes
- No crisis: 38 episodes
- Noncore change medians: 6.215 vs 2.692

The results do not establish a consistent protective
or amplifying role for noncore funding.

## Methodological boundaries

- No proven causal crisis transmission mechanism
- No validated abnormal-risk threshold
- No independent out-of-sample validation for joint signals
- Country and global-crisis observations may be dependent
- Retrospective JST crisis labels and revised data
- Archived CSV selection and missingness
- Small number of independent crisis events
- Noncore measure requires source-definition audit
- No GCF Production modifications

## Next research question

Do bank capital buffers or funding structure distinguish
otherwise comparable credit/funding/housing expansions
that culminate in crisis from those that do not?

Require adequate temporal ordering, country/era composition
checks, and episode independence before causal claims.

The aim remains an independently validated,
conditional cross-crisis transmission model.
