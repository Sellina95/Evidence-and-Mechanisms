# CASE-002 — V0 Closure

## Research Question

Which existing economic / financial mechanisms are most consistent with movements in U.S. long-term Treasury yields, when do they appear to work, and where do they break down?

## V0 Evidence Completed

### 1. Taylor / reaction-function baseline

The real-time Taylor-style baseline provides supporting evidence for policy-path analysis but does not strongly explain quarter-to-quarter 10Y yield changes in the tested sample.

### 2. Daily competing-mechanism evidence

The frozen daily first pass showed meaningful association between 10Y changes and several components / curve diagnostics, with no single series establishing a unique causal explanation.

### 3. Event-conditioned daily evidence

- Clean daily events: 113
- POLICY_PATH: 15 (13.3%)
- INFLATION_COMPENSATION: 22 (19.5%)
- TERM_PREMIUM_LONG_END: 19 (16.8%)
- MIXED: 45 (39.8%)
- INCONCLUSIVE: 12 (10.6%)

The largest V0 category is MIXED. This is evidence against treating U.S. 10Y yield moves as the product of one universal mechanism.

### 4. Recent intraday confirmation

- At least one intraday ETF proxy available: 10 events
- Complete SHY / IEF / TLT / TIP coverage: 6 events
- Partial coverage: 4 events

This recent intraday layer is supportive only because the historical coverage is short and some ETF observations are missing.

## V0 Conclusion

CASE-002 V0 supports a state-dependent, multi-mechanism interpretation of U.S. long-term Treasury yield movements.

Observed 10Y moves can be associated with different combinations of:

- policy-path-sensitive / real-rate repricing,
- inflation-compensation repricing,
- long-end / term-premium repricing,
- and mixed configurations in which multiple channels move together.

The evidence does not support a claim that one mechanism universally explains 10Y Treasury yield movements.

## What V0 Does NOT Establish

- The daily event study is not strict causal identification.
- DGS2 is not a direct measure of Fed expectations.
- T10YIE is not pure expected inflation.
- ACM term premium is model-estimated.
- DGS30 is not a pure term-premium measure.
- The recent intraday ETF sample is too short to replace historical daily evidence.
