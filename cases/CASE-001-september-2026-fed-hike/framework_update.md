# CASE-001 — Framework Update

## Previous Framework

The initial baseline was:

Fed tightening  
→ U.S. interest rates rise  
→ USD strengthens  
→ financial conditions tighten  
→ risk assets weaken  
→ EM currencies face depreciation pressure.

This remains a useful first-order framework, but CASE-001 shows that it is incomplete.

## Framework Deviation

The September 2026 episode demonstrated that a hawkish policy shock can affect different parts of the yield curve through opposing channels.

A more complete transmission structure is:

Fed tightening  
→ expected short-rate path rises  
→ front-end tightening pressure

while simultaneously:

credible anti-inflation commitment  
→ long-horizon policy / inflation uncertainty declines  
→ term premium may compress  
→ long-end yields may stabilize or decline.

External shocks can reinforce or offset these channels.

In CASE-001:

energy-supply risk eased  
→ macro and inflation tail risk declined  
→ long-duration risk compensation may have compressed further.

## Updated Framework

A hawkish Fed decision does not imply that every maturity must reprice in the same direction.

Long-term yields should instead be decomposed conceptually into:

**expected future short-rate path + term / risk premium**

Therefore:

**hawkish Fed ≠ all Treasury yields necessarily rise.**

A policy decision can simultaneously:

- raise expected short rates;
- strengthen anti-inflation credibility;
- reduce long-horizon uncertainty;
- compress term premium;
- lower long-end real yields;
- reduce valuation pressure on duration-sensitive risk assets.

## Research Rule Added

When a policy event produces an apparently contradictory cross-asset response:

1. separate front-end and long-end rates;
2. decompose nominal yields into real yield and inflation compensation;
3. inspect term-premium estimates where available;
4. test positioning rather than assuming position unwind;
5. identify simultaneous non-policy shocks;
6. do not treat the event as a single treatment when multiple shocks overlap.

## Remaining Unknown

CASE-001 cannot identify the exact contribution of Fed credibility, energy-risk relief, and market positioning to the decline in term premium.

That uncertainty is retained rather than replaced with a forced narrative.
