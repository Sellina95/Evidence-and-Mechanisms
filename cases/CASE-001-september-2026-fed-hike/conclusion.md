# CASE-001 — Conclusion

## Research Question

Why did the September 2026 Fed rate increase produce a cross-asset response that diverged from a simple tightening framework, particularly the approximately 7 bp decline in the 10-year real yield on September 17?

## Conclusion

The September 16–17 episode cannot be explained as a simple reaction to the rate increase itself.

The 25 bp hike was already heavily anticipated before the decision, while Fed communication remained hawkish and continued to support expectations of further tightening.

From September 16 to September 17:

- the 10-year nominal Treasury yield declined approximately 7 bp;
- the 10-year real yield declined approximately 7 bp;
- 10-year inflation compensation was approximately unchanged;
- the estimated 10-year Treasury term premium declined approximately 6 bp.

This combination is most consistent with compression in long-duration risk compensation rather than a dovish policy surprise or a further decline in inflation expectations.

The evidence is consistent with multiple contributing mechanisms:

1. clearer Fed anti-inflation commitment reducing long-horizon policy uncertainty;
2. lower interest-rate volatility following the policy decision;
3. easing energy-supply tail risk as oil prices reversed lower;
4. simultaneous repositioning across the Treasury futures complex.

Treasury futures prices rose on September 17 while open interest also increased across the 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, and Ultra-10Y December 2026 contracts.

This weakens a simple broad short-covering explanation and indicates that new gross positions were being created during the rally.

Open interest cannot identify the direction or economic purpose of those positions.

## Identification Limit

The available evidence does not permit a clean decomposition of the approximately 6 bp decline in estimated term premium into:

- Fed-credibility effects;
- lower rate uncertainty;
- energy-supply risk relief;
- positioning or relative-value activity;
- other technical factors.

The precise causal split therefore remains unknown.

## Final Assessment

**The strongest evidence indicates that the September 17 decline in the 10-year real yield was associated primarily with compression in long-duration risk compensation, not with a dovish Fed repricing or a further decline in inflation expectations.**

The episode is therefore best understood as a multi-mechanism repricing rather than a single-policy-shock response.

**Identification status: PARTIAL.**
