# CASE-001 — Competing Hypotheses

## Research Question

Why did the initial tightening response to the September 2026 FOMC decision partially reverse within one trading day?

The objective is not to find a plausible narrative.

The objective is to distinguish among competing mechanisms using evidence that could weaken or falsify each explanation.

---

## H1 — Pre-Pricing / Position Unwind

### Mechanism

Markets had already incorporated substantial expectations of tighter monetary policy before the FOMC decision.

The initial hawkish response therefore reflected confirmation of the decision, while the subsequent reversal reflected limited incremental policy surprise and the unwinding of pre-existing positions.

### Expected Evidence

If H1 is important:

- meaningful hawkish repricing should already be visible before September 16;
- market-implied policy expectations should have anticipated the rate increase;
- USD and/or rates positioning should have been materially tilted toward further tightening;
- the reversal should occur without a comparable dovish change in the Fed's policy signal.

### Weakening / Falsification Evidence

H1 would be weakened if:

- the hike or future tightening path was largely unexpected before the meeting;
- positioning was neutral or positioned in the opposite direction;
- the post-FOMC reversal followed a genuinely dovish policy-information shock;
- or the reversal was better synchronized with a separate macro shock.

### Evidence — Pre-FOMC Policy Pricing

Evidence available before the September 16 decision indicates that the immediate rate increase was already heavily anticipated.

- A September 14 Reuters poll found that 86 of 101 economists (85%) expected a 25 bp increase.
- Interest-rate futures priced approximately a 90% probability of the increase on September 14.
- Immediately before the decision on September 16, futures pricing implied approximately a 92.5–93% probability of a 25 bp increase.
- Swap markets had also priced roughly 60 bp of additional tightening through year-end.
- Contemporaneous market participants characterized the 25 bp increase itself as largely priced in.

This supports the **pre-pricing component** of H1.

However, this evidence does not yet establish that the subsequent reversal was caused by position unwinding. Direct positioning evidence and the evolution of expected future policy rates remain necessary.

**H1 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — pre-pricing established; unwind mechanism unproven.**
---

## H2 — Fed Credibility / Long-Run Inflation-Risk Repricing

### Mechanism

The rate increase raised near-term policy expectations but simultaneously increased confidence that the Federal Reserve would contain inflation.

This could produce different reactions across the yield curve:

Fed tightening
→ front-end policy expectations remain restrictive
→ long-run inflation risk declines
→ long-end yields fall relative to short-end yields
→ financial conditions partially ease
→ risk assets recover.

### Expected Evidence

If H2 is important:

- the yield curve should flatten following the decision;
- front-end yields should initially rise or remain relatively elevated;
- long-end yields should outperform the front end;
- inflation compensation and/or long-run inflation-risk measures should decline;
- contemporaneous market commentary should identify inflation credibility as a relevant mechanism.

### Weakening / Falsification Evidence

H2 would be weakened if:

- the entire yield curve simply moved together;
- long-end yields did not outperform the front end;
- inflation expectations rose materially;
- or the reversal occurred primarily because growth expectations deteriorated rather than inflation risk falling.

### Evidence — Inflation Compensation and Curve Response

Market-based inflation compensation moved in the direction predicted by H2 around the FOMC decision.

| Date | 5Y Breakeven Inflation | 10Y Breakeven Inflation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sep 15 | 2.41% | 2.38% |
| Sep 16 | 2.35% | 2.33% |
| Sep 17 | 2.32% | 2.33% |
| Sep 18 | 2.31% | 2.33% |

From September 15 to the September 16 FOMC:

- 5Y breakeven inflation declined approximately 6 bp.
- 10Y breakeven inflation declined approximately 5 bp.
- The nominal Treasury curve simultaneously flattened, with the 2Y yield rising substantially more than longer maturities.

This pattern is directionally consistent with a combination of tighter near-term policy expectations and lower longer-run inflation compensation.

However, the evidence does not identify Fed credibility as the causal mechanism. Breakeven inflation measures contain risk and liquidity components, and contemporaneous energy-price movements may also explain part of the decline.

### Real-Yield Decomposition

The nominal 10-year Treasury response concealed materially different movements in real yields and inflation compensation.

| Date | 10Y Nominal Yield | 10Y Real Yield | Approx. Inflation Compensation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sep 15 | 5.00% | 2.62% | 2.38% |
| Sep 16 | 5.01% | 2.68% | 2.33% |
| Sep 17 | 4.94% | 2.61% | 2.33% |
| Sep 18 | 5.01% | 2.68% | 2.33% |

The September 16 FOMC-day move combined an approximately 6 bp increase in the 10-year real yield with an approximately 5 bp decline in inflation compensation, leaving the nominal 10-year yield almost unchanged.

The September 17 decline in the nominal 10-year yield was different. Inflation compensation was approximately unchanged, while the 10-year real yield declined about 7 bp.

This weakens a simple interpretation in which the September 17 long-end rally was driven primarily by a further decline in inflation expectations following increased Fed credibility.

The September 18 reversal also cautions against a simple real-rate explanation: the 10-year real yield rose approximately 7 bp while equities continued to advance.

The evidence therefore suggests that neither the inflation-compensation channel nor the real-rate valuation channel alone explains the full post-FOMC cross-asset sequence.

**H2 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — predicted curve and inflation-compensation patterns are present; causal attribution to Fed credibility remains unproven.**

---

## H3 — Non-Fed Macro Shock / Oil-Inflation Reversal

### Mechanism

The post-FOMC reversal may not have been primarily caused by monetary policy.

A decline in oil prices or another macro shock could have reduced near-term inflation concerns and independently produced:

oil / inflation pressure ↓
→ Treasury yields ↓
→ USD pressure ↓
→ equity valuations ↑.

### Expected Evidence

If H3 is important:

- the timing of the reversal should align closely with the external macro shock;
- oil prices and inflation-sensitive market measures should move materially;
- yields and risk assets should react after that shock rather than solely around Fed communication;
- similar cross-asset behavior may appear outside FOMC events when comparable shocks occur.

### Weakening / Falsification Evidence

H3 would be weakened if:

- oil or other candidate macro variables moved too little to explain the repricing;
- the reversal began before the external shock;
- the strongest movements were concentrated around Fed communication;
- or the yield-curve pattern is difficult to explain through the external shock alone.

### Evidence — Energy Shock and Reversal

Energy prices moved materially around the post-FOMC reversal and therefore cannot be excluded as an independent driver.

- Oil prices declined for a second consecutive session on September 17 as equities and bonds rebounded.
- Contemporary market reporting explicitly linked lower oil prices and lower Treasury yields with the recovery in risk assets.
- On September 18, Brent settled at $104.87 per barrel, down 0.93%, while WTI settled at $100.30, down 1.58%.
- The decline partly reflected easing concerns about some Middle East supply disruptions.

The broader context is also important. Before the FOMC meeting, elevated energy prices had contributed to inflation concerns and to the market repricing toward tighter monetary policy.

This creates a possible feedback structure:

energy shock
→ inflation concern
→ tighter policy expectations
→ Fed tightening

followed by:

partial energy-price reversal
→ reduced inflation pressure
→ lower yields
→ partial risk-asset recovery.

However, oil was not the only contemporaneous mechanism. Market reporting also identified short-covering, profit-taking, and increased confidence in the Fed's inflation response.

The available evidence therefore does not establish that the oil reversal was the dominant cause of the post-FOMC market reversal.

**H3 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — an external energy-price reversal was present and directionally consistent with the market move; causal dominance remains unproven.**

---


## Current Status

H1: UNTESTED  
H2: UNTESTED  
H3: UNTESTED

No hypothesis is currently preferred.

Additional hypotheses may be added only when motivated by evidence rather than narrative convenience.

# Status Update — 2026-09-20

## H1 — Pre-Pricing / Position Unwind

### Updated Evidence

The pre-pricing component remains strongly supported: the September 16 rate increase itself was heavily anticipated before the decision.

However, the simple broad position-unwind mechanism is weakened by Treasury futures evidence.

On September 17, December 2026 Treasury futures rose across the 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, and Ultra-10Y contracts while open interest also increased across all four maturities.

This pattern indicates that the rally was not simply a contraction of outstanding positions. Some short covering may have occurred, but new gross positions were simultaneously being established.

### Updated Status

**H1 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — pre-pricing established; simple broad short-covering / liquidation mechanism weakened.**

---

## H2 — Fed Credibility / Long-Run Risk Repricing

### Updated Evidence

The original H2 mechanism requires refinement.

The September 17 decline in the 10-year nominal yield was approximately 7 bp and was almost entirely accounted for by a decline in the 10-year real yield. Ten-year inflation compensation was approximately unchanged.

Therefore the September 17 rally was not primarily driven by a further decline in inflation expectations.

However, the estimated 10-year Treasury term premium declined from approximately 0.67% on September 16 to 0.61% on September 17, a decline of approximately 6 bp.

This closely matched most of the same-day decline in the nominal 10-year Treasury yield.

The evidence is therefore more consistent with compression in long-duration risk compensation than with a simple inflation-expectation channel.

Possible contributing mechanisms include clearer Fed anti-inflation commitment, lower rate uncertainty, and lower interest-rate volatility.

The precise causal contribution of Fed credibility cannot be separately identified.

### Updated Status

**H2 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — the simple inflation-compensation channel is weakened, while a broader long-duration risk / term-premium compression channel is strongly supported by the observed decomposition. Causal attribution to Fed credibility remains incomplete.**

---

## H3 — Non-Fed Macro Shock / Energy-Supply Reversal

### Updated Evidence

Energy prices reversed sharply during the FOMC event window as Saudi supply concerns partially eased.

This creates a material confounding shock.

The September 16 decline in inflation compensation cannot be cleanly attributed to Fed credibility because the oil reversal occurred during the same window.

On September 17, however, 10-year inflation compensation was approximately unchanged while the 10-year real yield declined approximately 7 bp.

Therefore a simple oil-to-inflation-expectations channel does not explain the September 17 long-end rally.

A broader mechanism remains plausible:

energy-supply tail risk declines
→ macro / inflation uncertainty declines
→ long-duration risk compensation declines.

This mechanism is consistent with the observed term-premium compression but cannot be separately identified from Fed-related effects.

### Updated Status

**H3 status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — energy-risk relief was contemporaneous and likely contributed to lower uncertainty, but it does not independently explain the September 17 real-yield decline.**

---

## Current Cross-Hypothesis Assessment

The September 16–17 episode is best understood as a multi-stage, multi-mechanism repricing rather than a single reaction to the rate increase.

The strongest evidence is that:

- the hike itself was heavily pre-priced;
- the Fed communication remained hawkish;
- the September 17 nominal-yield decline was almost entirely a real-yield move;
- inflation compensation was approximately unchanged on September 17;
- Treasury futures rallied while open interest increased across maturities;
- the estimated 10-year term premium declined approximately 6 bp on September 17;
- energy-supply risk was simultaneously easing.

The available evidence does not permit a clean decomposition of the term-premium decline into Fed-credibility, energy-risk, positioning, or other technical components.

**Overall identification status: PARTIAL. UNKNOWN remains valid for the exact causal split.**
