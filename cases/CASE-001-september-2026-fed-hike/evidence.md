# CASE-001 — Evidence Synthesis

## Objective

Consolidate the evidence that discriminates among the competing explanations for the September 16–17, 2026 post-FOMC cross-asset reversal.

The purpose is not to force a single narrative. Evidence that weakens an initially plausible hypothesis is retained.

---

## 1. Policy Pre-Pricing

The September 16 rate increase itself was heavily anticipated before the decision.

This supports the pre-pricing component of H1.

However, pre-pricing does not by itself establish that the September 17 Treasury rally was caused by broad position liquidation.

**Finding:** Pre-pricing supported; unwind mechanism requires separate evidence.

## 3. Treasury Futures Positioning

December 2026 Treasury futures showed rising prices and rising open interest across maturities on September 17.

| Contract | Sep 17 Price Change | Sep 16 OI | Sep 17 OI | OI Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2Y ZTZ26 | +0.07% | 4,393,211 | 4,406,879 | +13,668 |
| 5Y ZFZ26 | +0.20% | 6,434,633 | 6,465,000 | +30,367 |
| 10Y ZNZ26 | +0.35% | 5,359,751 | 5,416,320 | +56,569 |
| Ultra-10Y TNZ26 | +0.47% | 2,573,866 | 2,587,811 | +13,945 |

The simultaneous increase in price and open interest across the Treasury complex is inconsistent with a simple broad position-liquidation story.

Some short covering may still have occurred, but the aggregate evidence indicates that new gross positions were also being established during the rally.

Open interest alone cannot identify whether those new positions were directional longs, directional shorts, hedges, basis trades, or other relative-value positions.

**Finding:** Simple broad short-covering explanation weakened.

## 4. 10-Year Treasury Term Premium

The estimated 10-year Treasury term premium declined materially around the FOMC event.

| Date | 10Y Term Premium |
|---|---:|
| Sep 14 | 0.70% |
| Sep 15 | 0.71% |
| Sep 16 | 0.67% |
| Sep 17 | 0.61% |

Changes:

- Sep 15 → Sep 16: approximately -4 bp
- Sep 16 → Sep 17: approximately -6 bp
- Sep 15 → Sep 17: approximately -10 bp

The approximately 6 bp decline in the estimated 10-year term premium on September 17 closely matched most of the approximately 7 bp decline in the nominal 10-year Treasury yield.

At the same time, 10-year inflation compensation was approximately unchanged and the 10-year real yield declined approximately 7 bp.

This is strong evidence that the September 17 long-end rally was associated with compression in long-duration risk compensation rather than a further decline in inflation expectations.

The term-premium estimate is model-based and refers to nominal Treasury term premium. It should not be interpreted as a direct one-for-one measure of the real term premium.

**Source:** ACM 10-Year Treasury Term Premium estimate, visualized by MacroMicro. Values manually transcribed from the public chart on 2026-09-20.

## 5. Energy-Supply Shock

The FOMC event did not occur in isolation.

Before and during the event window:

- elevated oil prices had contributed to inflation concerns;
- Saudi supply disruptions increased energy tail risk;
- information about alternative Saudi crude-routing arrangements subsequently reduced some supply-disruption concerns;
- oil prices reversed sharply lower on September 16 and continued lower on September 17.

This creates an important identification problem.

The September 16 decline in inflation compensation cannot be attributed cleanly to Fed credibility because the oil reversal occurred during the same event window.

On September 17, however, 10-year inflation compensation was approximately unchanged while the 10-year real yield fell approximately 7 bp.

Therefore a simple:

oil decline
→ inflation expectations decline
→ nominal yield decline

mechanism does not explain the September 17 move.

A broader channel remains plausible:

energy-supply tail risk declines
→ macro / inflation uncertainty declines
→ long-duration risk compensation declines

This channel cannot be separately identified from Fed-related policy and credibility effects.

---

## 6. Rate-Uncertainty Evidence

Contemporaneous market evidence showed lower interest-rate volatility after the FOMC event.

This is directionally consistent with a decline in long-duration risk compensation.

However:

- lower rate volatility is not identical to lower term premium;
- term premium is model-estimated;
- the causal contribution of Fed credibility, energy-risk relief, and other technical or positioning effects cannot be separately identified.

---

## Evidence Summary

The evidence weakens several simple explanations:

- **Dovish Fed repricing:** weakened.
- **Further inflation-expectation compression on Sep 17:** weakened.
- **Broad short-covering / position liquidation:** weakened.
- **Growth scare:** weakened.
- **TIPS auction as primary catalyst:** weakened.
- **Oil decline as a complete explanation:** weakened.

The strongest observed association is:

**September 17 long-end rally
→ approximately -7 bp in 10Y nominal yield
→ approximately -7 bp in 10Y real yield
→ approximately unchanged inflation compensation
→ approximately -6 bp in estimated 10Y term premium.**

This pattern is most consistent with compression in long-duration risk compensation.

The likely contributing mechanisms include:

1. clearer Fed anti-inflation commitment and lower long-horizon policy uncertainty;
2. lower interest-rate volatility;
3. easing energy-supply tail risk.

The relative causal contribution of these mechanisms cannot be separately identified from the available evidence.

**Current conclusion: MULTI-MECHANISM / PARTIALLY IDENTIFIED.**
